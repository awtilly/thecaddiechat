Boca West Country Club has been a fixture of South Florida golf since the early 1970s, its four courses threading through one of the state's largest private residential communities in Boca Raton. Course No. 1 — the flagship layout and the one members reach for first on a clear winter morning — has now entered its most comprehensive renovation in a generation.

The work began quietly last fall, with construction crews starting on the back nine while the front remained open for play. By early spring the full course was taken offline, and the scope of what's being done is becoming clearer.

## What's Changing

The greens are the centerpiece. The existing surfaces had accumulated decades of organic matter and subtle settling, producing inconsistencies that agronomists describe as "layering" — a condition where different soil horizons create unpredictable moisture movement and root zones that never quite reach their potential. The renovation involves stripping and rebuilding the greens profiles to USGA specification, with a bentgrass variety selected for South Florida's shoulder seasons.

Bunkers are being rebuilt as well, with drainage improved at each complex and faces reshaped to reflect more intentional strategic angles. Several bunkers that had been repositioned in previous eras are returning to positions closer to their original placement. The renovation architect has been working from aerial photographs and course cards going back to the late 1970s.

<div class="pull-quote">The goal isn't to make it harder — it's to make it honest again. Bunkers that actually catch the shots they're supposed to catch.</div>

The turfgrass program is also being overhauled. Fairways will be resodded with a Bermuda variety with finer leaf texture, and the rough specification is being reconsidered to better manage Boca West's historically aggressive summer grow-in.

## The Timeline

Club management has communicated a target reopening of Course No. 1 in October, ahead of the winter season that drives the highest demand from both members and their guests. Whether the grow-in cooperates with that schedule depends on how much heat the summer delivers — a variable that renovation architects in South Florida know better than to promise.

## What It Means for Members

During the closure, the club's other three courses remain available, and the membership has reportedly been supportive of the project. Long-tenured members often remember Course No. 1 before the various adjustments of the past two decades, and there is genuine anticipation about recovering conditions they associate with the layout's better years.

For a community-based club of Boca West's size, this kind of investment signals confidence in the long game — not just in the golf, but in the value proposition of a private residential community built around it.
